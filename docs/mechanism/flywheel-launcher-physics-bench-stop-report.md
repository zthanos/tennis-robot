# Isolated flywheel-launcher physics bench — pre-test stop report

Date: 2026-08-25

## Decision

The requested launch trials, RPM sweep, nip sensitivity study, differential
speed trials and trajectory map were **not run**. Two explicit stop conditions
are present before a defensible baseline can be established:

1. The simulated tennis ball is a rigid sphere, while the nominal 58 mm nip
   requires 8 mm of diametral overlap for a 66 mm ball. Gazebo's constraint
   penetration is not a tennis-ball deformation model.
2. Neither launcher geometry currently available to the simulator is an exact
   representation of the authoritative CAD launcher. The historical isolated
   launcher uses 40 mm wheels and a single 280 x 508 x 35 mm frame. The newer
   compact launcher uses the correct 50 mm wheel width and two 8 mm plates, but
   its plates are 258 mm wide rather than the CAD-derived 314 mm.

Changing the ball/contact model, nip, cradle or launcher selection before
measuring a baseline would violate the task's no-silent-tuning and
no-redesign-before-report requirements. The existing 2026-08-23 experiment is
supporting evidence only: the rigid 58 mm nip ejected the ball laterally, and a
64 mm hypothesis did not demonstrate useful flywheel acceleration.

## A. Bench architecture

No isolated physics bench exists in the repository. The existing
`option-a-launch` variant removes the intake, but it still generates the whole
robot URDF and selects the historical `side_by_side_flywheel_launcher`; it is
not the requested launcher-only world with a deterministic injector.

A valid future bench must contain only a fixed datum, CAD-matched cradle, two
flywheel links/joints/collisions, flywheel controller, one ball, ground and a
ground-truth injector/recorder. It must not use the feeder event, intake,
basket, perception, navigation or Throwing Mode.

## B. Ball model

Source: `gazebo/worlds/tennis_court.sdf`.

- Diameter: 66 mm (sphere radius 0.033 m).
- Mass: 0.058 kg.
- Inertia: diagonal `2.5e-5 kg m^2` on all axes. This is close to the
  `2/5 m r^2 = 2.526e-5 kg m^2` value for a uniform solid sphere, not a hollow,
  deformable tennis ball.
- Friction: ODE `mu=0.5`, `mu2=0.5`.
- Restitution: not specified on the collision; therefore it is engine/default
  behaviour, not a measured tennis-ball value.
- Contact tuning: no ball-specific contact stiffness, damping, ERP, CFM,
  slip, contact depth or maximum correcting velocity is specified. The world
  sets global ODE `cfm=0`, `erp=0.2`, and
  `contact_max_correcting_vel=2.0 m/s`.
- Compliance/deformation: none. Collision is one rigid sphere. There is no
  shell, pneumatic response, deformation state, calibrated force-deflection
  curve or hysteresis/loss model.

Consequently, solver penetration cannot be interpreted as physical ball
compression, and contact force/energy-transfer results at the current nip
would not be physically credible.

## C. Flywheel model

Authoritative CAD inputs in `cad/flywheel-launcher-v0/params.scad` are:

- diameter 200 mm;
- width 50 mm;
- centre distance 258 mm;
- nip 58 mm;
- pitch 20 degrees;
- edge round 6 mm, explicitly a visual hint rather than a tyre profile.

The historical simulated launcher in
`urdf/components/flywheel_launcher.urdf.xacro` has:

- 200 mm diameter, **40 mm width**, 0.30 kg per wheel;
- solid-cylinder inertia generated by `cylinder_inertial`:
  axial `0.0015 kg m^2`, transverse approximately `0.00079 kg m^2`;
- finite-cylinder collision geometry with square edges; the 6 mm CAD visual
  edge-round hint is not represented;
- friction `mu1=2.0`, `mu2=2.0`; restitution unspecified;
- continuous joints, local axis `0 0 1`, damping `0.002 N m s/rad`, nominal
  limit 320 rad/s and nominal effort limit 6 N m;
- a single 280 x 508 x 35 mm frame collision, rather than the CAD cradle.

The newer compact launcher in `compact_mechanics.urdf.xacro` has 200 x 50 mm,
0.40 kg wheels (axial inertia `0.0020 kg m^2`) and two 256 x 258 x 8 mm plate
collisions. The CAD `side_plate` is 256 x 314 x 8 mm because
`2 * (wheel_d/2 + cradle_margin) + nip_gap = 314 mm`. Thus this model is closer
but still not CAD-exact, and it is embedded in compact integration that this
task explicitly excludes.

Control uses a 100 Hz forward velocity controller through
`gz_ros2_control/GazeboSimSystem`. Existing evidence reports exact measured
tracking of `+100/-100 rad/s`. There is no motor torque-speed curve, bus
voltage, current limit, ESC, thermal model, shaft compliance or power limit.
It must therefore be treated as an operationally ideal simulated velocity
source, not evidence that a D5065 or any physical motor can sustain launch
load.

## D. Nip/compression geometry

- Wheel centre distance: `200 + 58 = 258 mm`.
- Geometric nip gap: 58 mm.
- Ball diameter: 66 mm.
- Nominal diametral interference: `66 - 58 = 8 mm`.
- Nominal interference fraction: `8 / 66 = 12.12%`.
- Symmetric radial interference at a perfectly centred nip: 4 mm per side.

This is a packaging definition of intended physical ball compression. In the
present rigid-body simulation it begins as incompatible geometry and is
resolved through numerical contact penetration/correction. Calling that
behaviour 8 mm of simulated ball compression would be misleading.

## Launcher orientation

The authoritative and simulated launcher baseline is pitched 20 degrees about
world +Y; no intake 35 degree mirrored-axis correction applies. The nominal
world launch unit vector is approximately `(0.93969, 0, 0.34202)`, i.e. 20
degrees above world +X. A wheel's positive local axis maps approximately to
`(-0.34202, 0, 0.93969)`; left/right command signs are opposite so both inner
surfaces drive toward local +X.

## E. Baseline launch

Not run: invalid ball compliance and non-authoritative simulated geometry make
the requested first-contact time, bilateral contact ordering/duration, force,
exit velocity, launch angle, spin and wheel disturbance non-validating data.
The previous feeder-equivalent experiment is specifically rejected as the
baseline because it was not a deterministic isolated injector test and did not
produce a clean bilateral launch.

## F. RPM sweep

Not run. No wheel-speed-to-exit-speed transfer function is claimed. For scale
only, the 0.1 m wheel radius converts 100, 180 and 320 rad/s to 10, 18 and
32 m/s surface speed; these are geometric conversions, not validated ball
speeds or physical motor operating points.

## G. Repeatability

Not run because there is no valid baseline trial.

## H. Nip sensitivity

Stopped before testing, as required. Varying the nip of a rigid sphere would
primarily characterize solver penetration, not tennis-ball compression. The
previous 64 mm trial remains an explicitly unaccepted simulation hypothesis.

## I. Differential-speed/spin capability

Not run because symmetric bilateral launch is not validated.

## J. Trajectory measurements

Not run. There is no validated exit state to propagate to first bounce.

## K. Failure modes observed or structurally exposed

- Rigid 58 mm nip: excessive penetration correction and lateral ejection in
  the prior experiment.
- 64 mm hypothesis: no meaningful flywheel-induced forward-speed increase and
  about 277 mm lateral deviation in the prior experiment.
- Asymmetric contact is likely to dominate when a rigid sphere is introduced
  to an overconstrained nip; it cannot be accepted as a launcher result.
- Historical isolated geometry can clip or redirect the ball using a frame
  shape absent from CAD.
- The current world has no dedicated bench instrumentation for wheel-contact
  ordering, contact force, penetration, tunnelling or solver convergence.

No clean capture, jam rate, cradle clipping rate or tunnelling limit is claimed
without the requested deterministic bench.

## L. Physics sanity assessment

The only defensible pre-test bound is geometric: for symmetric equal-speed
wheels with no stored elastic energy or external injector work, ball exit
speed should remain physically consistent with wheel surface speed and energy
transfer losses. The current model cannot quantify that transfer credibly
because it omits the ball's dominant compliance/loss mechanism and applies
velocity commands without a physical motor power model. No measured result is
accepted, and no parameters were tuned to force an expected outcome.

## M. Simulation-versus-physical limitations

- **SIMULATION_KINEMATIC_CAPABILITY:** pending; a CAD-matched isolated bench
  and a declared/calibrated compliant contact representation are required.
- **PHYSICAL_MOTOR_POWER_VALIDATION:** pending; the present controller cannot
  establish motor torque, current, power, thermal capacity or recovery between
  balls.
- Aerodynamic drag, Magnus force, felt interaction, ball pressure/temperature,
  wheel tyre compliance and speed-dependent losses are not represented in the
  audited ball/launcher definitions.
- A future compliant approximation must be explicitly documented and
  calibrated. It must not be introduced as silent tuning.

## N. Launcher module integration contract

Not ready. Provisional CAD datums that must remain fixed during the next gate
are: launcher origin `(560, 0, 215) mm` at the nip in the historical world
placement, pitch 20 degrees, wheel centres `(0, +/-129, 0) mm` in the
launcher nip frame, 58 mm nip and local +X launch direction. These are not a
validated integration contract.

Static/swept envelopes, required entry/exit/trajectory keep-outs, module mass,
COM and inertia must be exported from one authoritative standalone launcher
definition. Current simulated mass is model-dependent: 3.60 kg for the
historical frame-plus-wheels model versus 4.63 kg for the compact
frame/motors-plus-wheels model. Neither value is accepted as the physical
launcher contract.

## O. Tests

No launch regression was added because a passing test over the current model
would institutionalize invalid physics. Required future automated gates remain:
CAD/Xacro geometry equality, collision presence, declared compliant-ball
contract, deterministic pose/twist reset, zero initial spin, RPM readiness and
symmetry, clean bilateral capture, plausible exit-speed/energy bound,
repeatability and ground-truth first-bounce tracking.

## P. Visual validation

Not produced. Images of solver-driven rigid overlap would supplement an
invalid model rather than validate it. The future frame sequence must show the
same instrumented accepted trial at pre-entry, first/bilateral contact, exit
and trajectory phases.
